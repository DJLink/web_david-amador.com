Software Developers are anxious and burning out over state of tech

October 16, 2026 · 9 min · 1855 words · David Amador

Everything seems broken in the software development world right now.

I’ve been thinking about this for a while, but it has been escalating very rapidly especially in the last few weeks, and I’m mostly dumping a chain of thoughts for my own personal diary.

Is it just me feeling this?

Every piece of software turned their attentions to only deploying AI functionalities, among tons of other things that need to be done, nobody is paying attention to the details, they resort to the first AI prompt for their questions as the “true source of knowledge”; devs talking about having their games and software cloned via decompilation, there’s a whole subset group of people who seems happy they can now just clone/steal other people’s work.

What is happening?

If you don’t feel something is off or unsettling, I would say now is a good time for you to stop reading, you won’t resonate with any of this, might as well spend your time differently.

Before we start, here’s some 🍪🍪🍪🍪 and some ☕.#

Ok, so I started writing this several weeks ago, but what prompted me to finally finish it now, was a combination of things, the recent flood of news about the whole decompilation thing, that had way more people in my close circle, either online or IRL freaking out in a way that I haven’t seen, and mostly, everywhere you go even if you want to avoid some topics, there it is. I never necessarily avoid a healthy discussion about it, but now you have mostly people scared and the side of people with the typical “lol every dev is cooked” conversation.

The other day I was lurking on a random coding stream, and a whole meltdown started about that, “people will copy your game, etc” that person was not even against/pro AI, but the spiral of news clearly got to him, it’s hard to avoid the whole “doom and gloom AI singularity” even if you try.

I had 2 students in last months emailing me if they should quit the field while they could because of what everyone and parents were telling them. Just a few years back I would have parents asking me about the opposite for their kids.

Many people are panicking, and yes thousands are losing their jobs, but it’s a mix of corporate greed, stock manipulation, speculation and well, the economy is shit. And then there’s obviously the AI angle, it’s not one thing, but a pressure cooker of many aspects in a scale never seen. Let’s also not forget the rapid scale people got hired during the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic, a lot of those companies are undoing the remote thing in any way they can.

Many think it’s game over for software development, or that our profession is gone, and for sure the AI companies are doing the best they can to plant that idea. It’s like banks hovering around students to create a bank/credit account with them. The sooner they get the addiction rolling, the best for them. It doesn’t mean the don’t need a bank account, but you should avoid the credit angle. AI might just be the same, the bad part is being dependent on them is bad.

It’s affecting everyone directly or indirectly!

I’ve been coding professionally for 20 years now, which has been an interesting place to be, I can still learn A LOT from older generation of software developers and teams I collaborate with, and also still absorb the bubble of creativity that comes with the younger generations, they are usually closer to the new shifts in programming paradigms/ideas, while hopefully also teaching them something in return.

But something has shifted for everyone, the big elephant in the room, the pandora box that has been opened, the agentic era has arrived, either we want it or not, regardless if we think it’s a good idea for the future or not. I don’t even want to talk about that part specifically, because it’s a much bigger topic, and one that I’m not well equipped to talk; So more on the psychological toll I feel it’s causing to me and people around me?

🖊️ Thousands of developers who built the foundations of what is currently modern software and infrastructures, are now seeing everything invaded by a waterfall of AI-generated code, with minimal supervision, replacing things that probably shouldn’t be (yet?), with little regard to future effects to software, security, environmental or human effects.

All in the name of what companies are calling better productivity. And that’s the thing, both things can be true, it can be (or become) in fact a good productive tool for sure, but not in the way that it’s being deployed.

This is where I think a lot of friction comes from too, I think 100% pro or 100% against AI is not good anymore, there’s a discussion of what should exist, which mechanisms should be placed to avoid future issues…

Given that, personally, I don’t believe it will go back into the box.

And one of the reasons I believe this, is because it’s already engrained in the non-tech world, random places like the supermarket I hear people saying “I asked gemini for X”. Or people countering something with “ok sure but I just asked ChatGPT bla bla”. So it’s the new “I googled it”. You can’t undo this unless there’s a catastrophe that would turn everyone against AI, and obviously nobody wants those consequences.

Don’t get me wrong, I’m all for people learning, but would you push a 1st year medic student operating a machine, to do surgery on a patient just because it had AI? Of course not, and that’s easy for anyone to understand.

But computer software is more opaque, it seems innocuous, “it’s just software, what’s the harm?”, but everything runs on software now, from factories, medical devices, communications, fridges, power plants.

And I think that’s a lot of what’s helping me personally to feel unease, and I’ve also heard many saying the same. Tech debt is already a problem with human-written code, sometimes because of the rapid development things are forced on, and not enough time or testing.

The conversation started with the “all jobs will be replaced”, to which a lot of people paid attention, but I feel like the careless ways things are being deployed and used; are leaving the rest of people who were just quietly observing so far, to a semi-panic mode.

It’s not necessarily the “my job will be replaced” thing, but that tech is shifting for the most part to a worse version of itself for now. Nobody is verifying things, just pushing issues to others, or AI tokens will solve it; even some people who use it are burning out a bit over the rapid development speed that leaves less margin for pause, consider and evaluate.

Was it a problem that software was slow to develop? Maybe.

Was quality the bigger problem bubbling up? Absolutely.

You see, AI will absolutely solve the first one, I agree, for sure, and it’s only going to get faster, but will it make things better? It depends on who you ask, for a exec that wants to “break fast ship fast, sell company profit”, this is the way, and why it’s happening at such break necking speed without considerations. From a maintenance perspective this might be a nightmare to maintain moving forward. Or not, who’s to say right?

Oh but AI will be so fast that it can iterate it every time there’s a bug!

Again, no one can know if that will happen, I could be here saying that it’s impossible and be proven wrong, or proven right, but meanwhile production things are being deployed in the hopes that even if there’s a problem “AI will fix it”. So I can’t claim they won’t, but nobody can claim they will, so we should be just very careful while it produces worse results “but faster!!”.

Everyone will end up using it directly or indirectly#

At this pace even if you code everything yourself, odds are the tools we use were made with it, if not the tools than the Operative System etc.

Sure we can go back and install older tools/OS to avoid all of that etc, but if someone is in such position, that they can choose the entirety of the pipeline, what they do and the speed they do it, than I would say the whole subject is of no concern to that person. Which sounds great not going to lie.

What to do?#

Honestly, murphy’s law my friend, nothing can be done, it’s out of our hands, which might help a little bit, we might agree, not agree, be a bit worried, but no point of stressing over “I could do something about it”. You can do something about you. Sorry if you were looking for some magic bullet point that would relax you completely, but I think the ride is still starting before it plateaus on whatever it will land and keep it together, there’s no need to panic.

If you are working, continue the work, providing value to your company/co-workers, try not to stress out as much as possible. Find things to vent out if the subject causes you some anxiety, for me writing this was sort of cathartic.

If you are looking for work, it sucks, but keep strong, searching, keep yourself busy, maybe learning a new language/tool while at it. Even if AI takes over stuff, there’s always value in knowing things. I think the “no one will need to know stuff” is highly exaggerated. I would say to be aware of what exists as well in terms of AI stuff even if you don’t use them. I know Go language exists, I just never learned it, that kind of thing.

Go grab yourself some candy, look at the sky, this too shall pass.
